About CasePath

Our Mission

Separation shouldn’t cost you everything.

CasePath gives Australians family law information and preparation tools — without replacing qualified legal advice.

Why CasePath exists

Separation shouldn’t cost you everything

Separation and divorce are stressful in every situation. Fear of the unknown. Confusion about what happens next. Worry about your children, your future, your finances, and whether you are about to make a mistake you cannot undo.

Every year, tens of thousands of Australians enter the family law system. Most have never seen the inside of a courtroom. They do not know which form to file, what a directions hearing is, or what the court means by “the best interests of the child.”

They are often overwhelmed, exhausted, and trying to make important decisions during one of the hardest periods of their lives.

For many people, the first instinct is to seek legal advice — and qualified lawyers play an essential role in strategy, advocacy, and representation. At the same time, the court process can be difficult to navigate without plain-English guidance about forms, timelines, and terminology.

The language is technical. The process is unfamiliar. The stakes are deeply personal.

CasePath exists to help close that gap.

We provide plain-English information, structured guidance, and practical tools that help people better understand the family law process, stay organised, and participate more confidently in their own matter.

We do not replace lawyers, legal advice, or the court process. We help people understand what is happening around them so they are not left completely in the dark.

The beginning Getting in

The beginning of separation is often the hardest part.

You do not know what you do not know. You are searching legal terms late at night, trying to work out whether you need an initiating application or a response, whether you are the applicant or the respondent, and whether you have already missed a deadline you did not even know existed.

CasePath is designed for that moment.

We provide:

  • plain-English explanations of legal terms and court processes
  • step-by-step guidance through different stages of proceedings
  • tools to help organise documents, timelines, and evidence
  • practical information about parenting matters, mediation, hearings, and filing requirements

The goal is simple: when you walk into a courtroom, mediation, or a lawyer’s office, you should have a clearer understanding of what is happening and why.

The middle Getting through

Family law matters are rarely resolved quickly.

Contested parenting proceedings can take many months, and sometimes years. During that time, people are expected to manage documents, deadlines, hearings, evidence, negotiations, parenting arrangements, work, finances, and the emotional pressure that comes with ongoing conflict.

The emotional toll of separation is real, and it is often underestimated.

CasePath is designed to help people stay organised and informed throughout that process.

Our platform includes tools to help users:

  • track the progress of their matter
  • securely store documents and evidence
  • prepare structured drafts and notes
  • understand procedural steps and court terminology
  • access mental health and support resources during proceedings

We believe that helping people feel more informed and organised can reduce confusion, improve communication, and support better decision-making during an already difficult time.

The end Getting out

The goal of family law proceedings should be resolution — not endless conflict.

Whether that resolution comes through agreement, mediation, consent orders, or final orders after a hearing, the end of proceedings should create stability and allow families to move forward.

CasePath encourages early resolution wherever it is safe and appropriate to do so.

We aim to help people:

  • better understand realistic outcomes
  • communicate more effectively
  • reduce unnecessary conflict
  • prepare more constructively for negotiation and mediation
  • focus on long-term stability, especially where children are involved

The family law process is not just about legal documents. It affects parents, children, relationships, finances, housing, mental health, and the shape of everyday life for years afterward.

Behind every file number is a real family trying to find a way forward.

At the centre Protecting the children

Children did not choose the separation. They did not create the conflict. But they often experience its consequences most deeply.

Every overheard argument, every disrupted routine, every prolonged dispute can affect a child’s sense of stability and security.

Australian family law places the best interests of the child at the centre of parenting decisions. Protection from harm is the paramount consideration, alongside the broader factors the court must consider when determining parenting arrangements.

Where it is safe and in a child’s best interests, children generally benefit from having a meaningful relationship with both parents.

At the same time, safety must always come first. Family violence, abuse, neglect, coercive control, and genuine risks to children must be taken seriously.

CasePath supports an approach that prioritises:

  • child-focused decision making
  • safety and wellbeing
  • reduced conflict
  • earlier resolution where possible
  • preserving healthy parent-child relationships where safe to do so

Children should not be turned into participants in adult conflict.

What we believe

Principles that guide how CasePath is built and how we hope to support people through separation.

Access to justice matters

People should be able to understand the legal process affecting their family — with confidence, preparation, and informed decision-making — whether they are working with a lawyer or preparing on their own.

Family court processes can feel intimidating when information is hard to find or understand. We believe access to clear information helps everyone make more informed decisions during separation — and that better organisation supports more effective use of professional legal advice where it is needed.

Clarity matters

People make better decisions when they understand what is happening, what their options are, and what the process may involve.

Fear and conflict often grow in uncertainty. We believe clearer information, structured guidance, and practical tools can help people feel more grounded, informed, and capable during separation and family law proceedings.

Mental health matters

Separation affects emotional wellbeing, decision-making, parenting, and communication. Supporting mental health is part of supporting people through the legal process.

Children come first

The wellbeing, safety, and stability of children should remain central throughout every stage of family law proceedings.

Meaningful relationships matter

Where it is safe and appropriate, children benefit from healthy relationships with both parents and from being protected from unnecessary conflict.

Safety is non-negotiable

No principle overrides the need to protect people from family violence, abuse, neglect, or genuine risk.

Resolution matters

We do not help people fight harder. We help people understand the process more clearly so they can move toward informed, constructive, and realistic outcomes.

Privacy matters

Information uploaded to CasePath is intended to remain private, secure, and under the user’s control.
