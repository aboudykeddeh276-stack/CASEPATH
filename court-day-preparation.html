Court Day Preparation Pack

Practical guidance on attending family court in Australia — what to bring, what to expect, and how to prepare for your hearing day.

Educational information only. This is not legal advice and does not generate court documents. Every case is different.

!
General information only

Court procedures vary by registry, matter type, and judicial officer. Comply with all Court Orders. Obtain legal advice where appropriate. If you are in immediate danger, call 000.

Reminder: This pack describes common experiences only. Hearings differ. Always follow directions given in your matter and seek independent legal advice about your situation.

Before Your Court Date

Practical steps that may help you feel more prepared before you attend court.

  • Confirm the date, time, and location of your hearing
  • Read every document you have filed
  • Read any documents filed by the other party
  • Organise documents in a logical order
  • Print copies if attending in person
  • Charge your phone fully
  • Arrange transport and allow extra time
  • Plan to arrive early
  • Dress neatly and respectfully
  • Bring water and any required medication

Printable preparation checklist

Use this checklist before your court date. Tick items as you complete them.

What to Bring

Bring what applies to your matter. The Court may request specific documents — check your directions and orders.

Arriving at Court

Security screening

Most courts require security screening. Allow extra time and follow staff directions.

Finding the courtroom

Check noticeboards, ask court staff, or use registry counters if you are unsure where to go.

Electronic court lists

Many registries display lists on screens. Confirm your matter number and courtroom.

Waiting outside

You may wait outside the courtroom until your matter is called. Be ready when your name or matter is announced.

Speaking with the other party

Only speak with the other party if it is safe and appropriate. Follow any protection orders or court directions.

Phone on silent

Turn your phone to silent before entering court areas.

Etiquette while waiting

Remain respectful, speak quietly, and avoid disruptive behaviour in public court areas.

Inside the Courtroom

People you may see in the courtroom include:

  • Judicial officer — the judge, registrar, or magistrate who conducts the hearing
  • Registrar — may assist with procedural matters
  • Court Officer — helps manage the courtroom
  • Lawyers — representing parties where applicable
  • Independent Children’s Lawyer (ICL) — may appear in parenting matters involving children
Where to sit

Sit where directed by court staff or the judicial officer. Follow seating arrangements in the courtroom.

When to stand

Stand when the judicial officer enters or leaves, and when directed.

When to speak

Wait until you are invited to speak. Do not interrupt the judicial officer or other parties.

How to address the Court

Address a judge as “Your Honour”. Registrars may also be addressed as “Your Honour” in many courts — follow local practice.

Taking notes

You may take notes unless directed otherwise. Note dates, directions, and orders carefully.

Speaking calmly

Speak clearly and calmly. Focus on facts and what the Court has asked.

What Happens at a First Hearing

At many first hearings, the Court is usually organising the case rather than deciding final outcomes. This is a general description only — not every hearing follows the same process.

The Court may, depending on your matter:

  • make interim orders
  • set timetables and filing deadlines
  • order mediation or family dispute resolution
  • order disclosure of documents
  • discuss subpoenas
  • list future hearings

Every case is different. Procedures vary. What happens at your hearing depends on your matter, the issues in dispute, and directions already made.

Questions You May Be Asked

The judicial officer may ask questions to understand your matter. Examples only — you may not be asked these exact questions:

  • What orders are you seeking today?
  • Why is this matter urgent?
  • Have documents been served?
  • What are the current parenting arrangements?
  • Have you attended mediation?
  • Are there any immediate safety concerns?
  • Is there anything preventing the matter progressing?

These are examples only. Questions vary by matter type, urgency, and what the Court needs to understand on the day.

Courtroom Etiquette

  • Remain respectful at all times
  • Do not interrupt the judicial officer or other speakers
  • Answer the question asked
  • Avoid arguing with the other party in the courtroom
  • Take notes of directions and dates
  • Ask for clarification if you do not understand something
  • Remain as calm as you can

After the Hearing

  • Read any Orders carefully as soon as you receive them
  • Record important dates and deadlines in your diary or calendar
  • Complete tasks before deadlines
  • Keep copies of all Orders in a safe place
  • Update your records and preparation notes
  • Begin preparing for the next stage of your matter

Comply with Court Orders. Failure to follow orders can have serious consequences. Obtain legal advice if you are unsure what an order requires.

Questions Before Leaving Court

If appropriate and you are invited to ask, examples of useful questions include:

  • What happens next?
  • When is my next hearing?
  • When are documents due?
  • Who serves these Orders?
  • Do I need to file anything else?

Ask when invited. If you are represented, your lawyer may ask on your behalf.
