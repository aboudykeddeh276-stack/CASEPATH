What List Is My Case In?

Plain-English explanations of common FCFCOA pathways — from standard case management to Lighthouse, the Evatt List, urgent matters and more.

This page provides general educational information only. It is not legal advice and cannot tell you which list your specific matter is in. Always check your notice of listing and the Commonwealth Courts Portal.

How to use this page

The Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia (FCFCOA) progresses matters through different pathways depending on complexity, urgency, risk and the issues in dispute. Your notice of listing is the practical source of truth for your matter.

Use the sections below to understand common pathway names you may see in orders, portal entries or Court information. Then open the linked glossary terms for deeper plain-English explanations.

Related hub: Case Management Pathway · Official starting point: fcfcoa.gov.au

Standard Case Management

What it is

The ordinary FCFCOA pathway for progressing a matter with directions, timetables, compliance checks and listings matched to the issues.

Who usually enters it

Many parenting and financial matters that are not allocated to a specialist high-risk list and are not treated as immediate duty/urgent listings.

What usually happens

A first return or directions hearing, dispute resolution where appropriate, further directions, readiness checks, and eventually interim or final hearings if needed.

Common misconceptions

That every case follows exactly the same hearing sequence and timetable.

What to expect

Read each notice of listing carefully. Prepare for the purpose of that event — mentions and compliance listings are usually not final trials.

Related glossary terms

Case Management, Case Management Pathway, First Return Hearing, C&R Hearing

FCFCOA website

Lighthouse

What it is

An early risk-identification pathway used in many parenting matters, often involving confidential Family DOORS screening.

Who usually enters it

Parenting matters invited into risk screening. Not every parenting matter enters Lighthouse.

What usually happens

Screening helps the Court identify risk and choose safer case management. Screening is not, by itself, the evidence in your case.

Common misconceptions

That completing Lighthouse screening automatically becomes sworn evidence or guarantees Evatt List entry.

What to expect

Complete screening carefully if invited. Continue preparing affidavits and Notices of Risk as required for hearings.

Related glossary terms

Lighthouse, Family DOORS, Risk Assessment, Family Violence

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA — Lighthouse

Evatt List

What it is

A specialist list for the highest-risk parenting matters, with intensive judicial management and earlier interventions.

Who usually enters it

Parenting matters assessed as involving serious safety risk, often after screening and risk notices.

What usually happens

Closer supervision, safety-focused interim steps, and specialist resources. Listing changes management — it does not itself finalise parenting orders.

Common misconceptions

That Evatt List entry means you have already won or lost the case.

What to expect

Follow directions closely. Prepare focused safety and parenting evidence for interim events.

Related glossary terms

Evatt List, Lighthouse, Judicial Registrar, Independent Children's Lawyer (ICL)

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA — Lighthouse / specialist pathways

Urgent Matters

What it is

Applications seeking prompt Court attention because of imminent risk or another time-critical issue.

Who usually enters it

Parties facing imminent harm risk, child retention/removal risk, or other genuinely time-critical problems.

What usually happens

Possible duty-list or urgent interim listing before a Judicial Registrar, Senior Judicial Registrar, or Judge.

Common misconceptions

That labelling a dispute 'urgent' guarantees the orders sought.

What to expect

File a short, factual urgency affidavit and proposed orders. Be ready on short notice.

Related glossary terms

Urgent Application, Interim Hearing, Duty List, Senior Judicial Registrar

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA website

Parenting Proceedings

What it is

Cases about children's living arrangements, time, communication and major long-term decisions.

Who usually enters it

Parents and other eligible applicants seeking or responding to parenting orders.

What usually happens

Best-interests analysis, possible risk screening, reports, ICL appointment in some cases, and staged case management.

Common misconceptions

That equal time or equal shared decision-making is automatic under current law.

What to expect

Keep evidence child-focused. Understand current best-interests factors and decision-making / parental responsibility rules.

Related glossary terms

Parenting Proceedings, Best Interests of the Child, Decision-making Responsibility, Child Impact Report

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA — Family Law

Financial Proceedings

What it is

Cases about property settlement and related financial issues after separation.

Who usually enters it

Parties seeking property or maintenance orders, sometimes alongside parenting proceedings.

What usually happens

Disclosure, valuations, dispute resolution, and case management toward interim or final determination if needed.

Common misconceptions

That the first return hearing will finalise the property pool.

What to expect

Prioritise complete disclosure and genuine resolution attempts before trial.

Related glossary terms

Financial Proceedings, Disclosure, Dispute Resolution Event, Genuine Steps Certificate

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA — Family Law

Contravention Proceedings

What it is

Proceedings alleging someone breached a family law order without a reasonable excuse.

Who usually enters it

Parties alleging or responding to alleged non-compliance with orders.

What usually happens

The Court examines the order, the alleged breach, any reasonable excuse, and what should happen next.

Common misconceptions

That contravention and recovery order pathways are always the same thing.

What to expect

Keep dated records of the order, the alleged breach, and communications.

Related glossary terms

Contravention Proceedings, Contravention, Recovery Order, Breach of Orders

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA — Family Law

Recovery Order Matters

What it is

Urgent pathways seeking orders to locate and/or recover a child who has not been returned or whose whereabouts are unknown.

Who usually enters it

Parties facing non-return, retention, or unknown whereabouts of a child.

What usually happens

Urgent listing may consider location orders, recovery orders, or both, depending on the facts.

Common misconceptions

That a location order automatically returns the child without further recovery steps.

What to expect

Act quickly with clear last-known facts. Choose the remedy that matches the problem.

Related glossary terms

Recovery Order, Location Order, Recovery Order Request, Child Abduction

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA — Family Law

Appeals

What it is

A separate pathway for challenging certain court decisions according to appeal rules and time limits.

Who usually enters it

Parties seeking to challenge an appealable decision — not every disagreement with an order is an appeal.

What usually happens

Appeal documents, possible stay applications, and listing in the appeal stream. Appeals are not a re-run of the whole trial as a fresh first hearing.

Common misconceptions

That an appeal is simply another chance to re-argue the same facts without legal error grounds.

What to expect

Check appeal time limits immediately and get legal advice about whether an appeal pathway is available.

Related glossary terms

Final Hearing, Reasons for Judgment, Court Order

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA website

Circuit sittings

What it is

Court sittings held on circuit in regional locations according to the Court's circuit calendar.

Who usually enters it

Matters allocated to a circuit registry or listed when the Court sits in that location.

What usually happens

Listings may be bunched into circuit weeks. Timetables can look different from metropolitan daily lists.

Common misconceptions

That circuit matters are informal or optional.

What to expect

Confirm the venue, date and judicial officer on your notice of listing and portal entry.

Related glossary terms

Case Management, First Return Hearing, Filing

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA website

Judge-managed matters

What it is

Matters where a Judge closely manages progression and/or hears contested issues allocated to judicial determination.

Who usually enters it

Often more complex, contested, or specialist-list matters — allocation is a Court decision.

What usually happens

Directions, interim hearings and/or final hearings before a Judge, sometimes after earlier registrar management.

Common misconceptions

That only Judges ever make important orders in FCFCOA matters.

What to expect

Prepare to the same high standard for Judge listings as for any contested hearing.

Related glossary terms

Final Hearing, Interim Hearing, Trial Management Hearing, Case Management Pathway

Official FCFCOA references

FCFCOA website

Registrar-managed matters

What it is

Matters progressed mainly by a Judicial Registrar or Senior Judicial Registrar through procedural and many interim listings.

Who usually enters it

A large proportion of early FCFCOA case management events.

What usually happens

First returns, directions, duty lists, compliance events and some interim determinations within delegated powers.

Common misconceptions

That registrar listings are informal or that directions are optional.

What to expect

Arrive with proposed directions or minutes. Registrar orders are binding within their powers.

Related glossary terms

Judicial Registrar, Senior Judicial Registrar, Directions Hearing, First Return Hearing
