Safety & protection

Will My Protection Order Work Interstate?

Educational information about national recognition of domestic and family violence protection orders across Australia.

This page provides general information only. It is not legal advice and does not recommend any course of action.

National recognition framework

Since 25 November 2017, domestic and family violence protection orders made in one Australian state or territory are automatically recognised in every other state and territory under the national recognition scheme.

This means an order made in NSW, Victoria, Queensland, the ACT, or elsewhere is generally enforceable interstate without needing to re-apply in the new location — police can treat it as if it were made locally.

Orders covered include (terminology varies by jurisdiction):

  • ADVO / APVO (NSW)
  • DVO (Queensland)
  • FVIO / PSIO (Victoria)
  • FVO / PPO (ACT)

See also: National Recognition of Protection Orders and Protection Order Terminology by Jurisdiction.

Interstate recognition in practice

If you move or travel to another state, your existing order should continue to protect you. Local police enforce the conditions as written on the order.

Carry a copy of your order

Keep a paper or secure digital copy. If police attend an incident, showing the order helps them identify conditions quickly.

Report breaches to local police

Contact 000 in an emergency. Breaching a recognised order is a criminal offence in the state where the breach occurs.

Conditions are read as written

National recognition does not change the wording of your order. Always follow the exact conditions on your document.

Practical considerations

  • Registration: Automatic recognition applies to eligible orders — you generally do not need to register separately, but keep proof of the order.
  • Parenting orders: If you also have Family Court parenting orders, check how they interact with protection conditions in your new location. Seek legal advice if they conflict.
  • Variations: To change conditions, you usually apply through the court that made the order or as directed by local legislation — not by informal agreement.
  • Firearms and licences: Weapons restrictions may apply automatically when an order is in force — check local rules if you hold licences in multiple states.
  • Support services: 1800RESPECT (1800 737 732) operates nationally; state-specific services are listed in the Protection & Safety Orders Centre.

Limitations

National recognition covers domestic and family violence protection orders under the cooperative scheme. It may not apply to every type of civil order in every circumstance.

  • Orders made before 25 November 2017 may have different recognition rules.
  • Some non-domestic orders (for example certain peace and good behaviour orders) are not part of the same scheme.
  • Enforcement details, police procedures, and court registries differ between jurisdictions.
  • This page cannot tell you whether a specific order is currently enforceable in your situation — that requires legal advice.
